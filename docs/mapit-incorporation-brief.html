Looking ahead

Signals that should bring you back to this decision

None of these are close right now — but each one meaningfully changes the calculus above, so it's worth knowing what to watch for rather than deciding on a fixed calendar date.

  1. MapIt starts handling money — escrow, paid listings, or any in-platform payment

    The moment MapIt touches a transaction (not just facilitates contact between buyer and seller), the "MapIt does not process payments" clause in the Terms of Service stops being true — and Arun's personal liability exposure stops being theoretical.

  2. Nagesh's role needs to become a real, documented stake

    A sole proprietorship has no mechanism to give Nagesh actual ownership — only an informal agreement outside any legal structure. If that's ever the intent, an LLP (partner) or Pvt Ltd (shares/ESOP) is the point where it becomes real.

  3. You want to raise money from anyone outside the two of you

    Angels and VCs invest in companies, not sole proprietorships. This is usually the single biggest forcing function for incorporating.

  4. MapIt formally hires — even one person

    Payroll, PF/ESI obligations, and employment contracts are far cleaner under a registered entity than under a personal proprietorship.

  5. User base grows past "people Arun and Nagesh know"

    Manual listing approval and a small trusted user base are effectively today's safety net against fraud/liability exposure. Once MapIt is open to the general public, that safety net thins — and so does the case for staying unincorporated.

Where this leaves you

Stay a sole proprietorship for now — revisit at the first trigger above, not on a deadline

At zero revenue, zero outside money, and a user base you both personally know, none of the real risks of unlimited liability or the real benefits of incorporation (fundraising, formal co-ownership, employee hiring) are live yet. The heavier annual compliance cost of an LLP or Pvt Ltd — CA/CS fees, mandatory filings — would be pure overhead against no corresponding benefit right now.

Converting a sole proprietorship into an LLP or Pvt Ltd later is a well-worn, routine path in India — it is not a one-way door, and delaying incorporation doesn't foreclose it. Decide again when one of the five triggers above actually happens, not before.
